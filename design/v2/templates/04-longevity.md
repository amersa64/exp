# Template 04 — Longevity

The "stay fit, age well" goal. The smallest segment by population, the most underserved by existing apps, and the easiest to do badly.

## Who picks this

The user who said *"I want to be strong enough to live well at 80."* Concretely:

- Often 50+ but not always (the Diane persona is the prototype)
- Sometimes never lifted; sometimes lifted long ago and stopped
- Cares about *what they can do*, not what they can lift
- Wants to avoid injury, falls, frailty
- Has read about sarcopenia or seen a parent decline
- Reads Peter Attia, Andy Galpin, NYT health column
- **Has been sold "wellness" garbage their whole life and is suspicious of it**

This last point matters: longevity users are pattern-matched to discount slick marketing. The template should be plainspoken, jargon-free, honest about what works and what doesn't.

Anti-personas:
- Wants to PR squat → Strength (and they probably should — strength IS longevity below 70)
- Wants to lose 30 lb → Conditioning
- Wants to "get jacked" → Hypertrophy

## What "longevity" means in this template

Peter Attia's "Centenarian Decathlon" framework gives the design constraint: train the things you'll need at 90 — get off the floor unassisted, carry groceries, walk briskly, balance on one foot, carry your bodyweight. The template trains the **movement patterns of independent living**, not the lifts of competitive sport.

Five capacities to train:

1. **Lower body strength** — squat to chair, step up, walk uphill
2. **Pushing capacity** — push up from the floor, push a heavy door
3. **Pulling capacity** — open a stuck jar, pull yourself up
4. **Carrying capacity** — groceries, suitcase, grandchild
5. **Stability + balance** — stand on one foot, walk a narrow path

Plus: **mobility** (range of motion) and **aerobic base** (walking, easy bike).

## Split structure

**2-3 days/week, full body, low impact.**

| Day | Workout | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Full Body — Strength | Lower + push + pull + carry |
| Wed | Full Body — Mobility + Balance | Easier strength + mobility flow + balance |
| Fri | Full Body — Strength | (variations of Monday) |

Or a 2-day version that drops Wednesday. The cadence is *less* — recovery matters more, accumulated wear is the enemy.

## Rep ranges

Higher than strength template, lower than hypertrophy. Focused on doing *more reps with good form* rather than chasing load:

- Compound lifts: **8-12 reps**, 2-3 sets, 90-120s rest
- Mobility / balance / carry: **time-based** (30s-60s holds, 30-60s walks)
- Aerobic: **20-30 min walks** at conversational pace

Conservative. Always conservative. A longevity user with a 70-year-old hip cannot afford the strength user's "push past the failure" mindset.

## Progression

**Glacial.** Add small loads or reps only when the previous progression has been *sustained for 3+ weeks without strain*. Most weeks, the program does not progress — and that is the point.

This is the hardest thing to communicate to longevity users who come from a "more is more" culture: the goal is not progression. The goal is *not deteriorating*. Holding a plank for 30 seconds every week for a year is the win.

The brain should explicitly call this out:

> *"same load this week. boring is the point. you're not deteriorating — that's the win at 58."*

## Days/week supported

- 1 → marginal; better than nothing; doesn't move the needle. Recommend 2.
- 2 → canonical for the older / more deconditioned user
- 3 → ceiling. Above this risks injury or recovery deficit.
- 4+ → discouraged. Route to a different template if they're young + capable.

## Time per session

- Default: **30 min** (including walking-around warm-up + cool-down)
- Compressed (20 min): drop the cool-down stretching
- Extended (45 min): add a full mobility flow before strength work

Shorter than other templates because compliance > volume. A 30-min routine done 50 weeks a year beats a 60-min routine done 12 weeks a year.

## Equipment branches

This template *expects* limited equipment:

| Equipment | What changes |
|---|---|
| Full gym | Cable rows, machines for assistance — easier on joints |
| Barbell+rack | Barbell goblet squat, RDL, OHP, row |
| Dumbbells | **Canonical for this template** — DBs are the safest, most flexible tool for older lifters |
| Bodyweight | Chair squat, wall pushup, doorway row, walking — totally viable |

Worth noting: the template *prefers* DB or bodyweight to barbell for true beginners. Barbell technique under load is a long learning curve, and a longevity user doesn't have decades to master it.

## Vocabulary — the biggest design constraint

The Diane persona's complaint: *"It assumes I know what a 'row' is."* This template uses **plain English** instead of gym vernacular:

| Gym word | Plain word |
|---|---|
| Squat | "Sit down to a chair and stand up" |
| Bench press | "Lying-down chest press" |
| Row | "Pull the weight toward your ribs" |
| Deadlift | "Pick up the weight off the floor with a straight back" — wait, no, longevity probably skips deadlifts |
| Overhead press | "Press the weight overhead" |
| RPE | (don't use) |
| 1RM | (don't use) |
| Set | "Round" |
| Rep | "Time" (as in "do this 10 times") |
| Superset | (don't use) |
| Drop set | (don't use) |
| Compound | (don't use) |

The voice spec already says no jargon. This template enforces it strictly. A longevity user should be able to read the entire app without needing to Google a single term.

## Special considerations

### Balance is a first-class movement

Other templates barely touch balance. This template programs it explicitly:

- Single-leg stand (eyes open → eyes closed progression)
- Heel-to-toe walk (forward, then backward)
- Single-leg deadlift (assisted at wall as needed)

Balance training reduces fall risk meaningfully for older adults — and falls are the #1 risk factor for late-life decline. Programming balance is not aesthetic, it's life-extending.

### Mobility is programmed, not "stretching"

Real mobility work — controlled articular rotations (CARs), thoracic openers, hip 90/90 — not the high-school PE static stretching. The exercise catalog has these but they're labeled obscurely. Worth adding cleaner names for this template.

### Recovery is the program

Most templates treat recovery as the absence of training. Longevity treats recovery as part of training:

- Walking on rest days is encouraged
- Sleep is tracked and called out (*"6 hours last night. easy day today."*)
- Stress markers (resting HR if available) shift the prescription

### No PR culture

The Becoming tab for a longevity user should de-emphasize:
- Numerical milestones (no "squat 1.5× bodyweight")
- Progression metrics (no "+5 lb this week")
- The mountain hero (or render it differently — a stable, full mountain, not a growing one)

And emphasize:
- Days trained this month
- Walks taken
- Months of consistency
- "How you feel" qualitative check-ins

This is a meaningful Becoming-tab variation by template — see composable-blocks.md for the layout.

## Sample week — dumbbells at home, 3 days

```
Monday — Full Body Strength (30 min)
  Warmup walk 5 min
  Sit-to-stand (chair squat) 10 reps × 2 rounds
  Wall pushup 8-10 reps × 2 rounds
  Bent-over row with 10 lb DBs 10 reps × 2 rounds
  Suitcase carry — 10 lb DB, 30 steps each side × 2 rounds
  Cool-down stretch 5 min

Wednesday — Mobility + Balance (30 min)
  Warmup walk 5 min
  Hip CARs 5 each direction × 2 sides
  Thoracic opener 8 reps × 2 sides
  Single-leg stand 30s × 2 sides (eyes open this month)
  Heel-to-toe walk 10 steps × 2 directions
  Easy chair squat 10 reps × 2 rounds
  Stretch 5 min

Friday — Full Body Strength (30 min)
  Warmup walk 5 min
  Step-up onto a low step 8 reps each leg × 2 rounds
  Doorway row 10 reps × 2 rounds
  Overhead press with 8 lb DBs 8 reps × 2 rounds
  Farmer carry 10 lb DBs, 40 steps × 2 rounds
  Stretch 5 min
```

Daily encouraged: 20-30 min walk outside.

## Milestones

These look different from other templates. Focused on *function*, not load:

1. **Single-leg stand for 30 seconds, eyes open** — month 1
2. **Sit-to-stand 10 reps without hands** — month 1-2
3. **Walk briskly for 30 min, twice in a week** — month 2-3
4. **Carry 15 lb dumbbells for 60 steps** — month 3-4
5. **6 months consistent** — the long-haul marker

No squat-bodyweight milestone. No bench-100-lb milestone. These would set the wrong frame.

## Adaptation rules

| Report | Brain action |
|---|---|
| Done, feels good | Hold load. Maybe add 1 rep next week. Maybe not. |
| Done, felt easy | Add 1 rep or 30s next week. Not load. |
| Done, felt hard | Hold for 2-3 weeks before considering progression |
| Partial (any reason) | Hold. No retry pressure. |
| Skipped | Hold. *"No worries. Walking still counts. Tomorrow's a fresh start."* |
| Pain reported | **STOP that exercise.** Don't substitute today. Surface the safety handoff: *"that's a doctor conversation."* |

The pain-stop is critical for this template. Other templates work around minor pain; this template stops. Reason: the cost of pushing through pain at 60 is much higher than at 30. Spec §4.4 safety boundary is most active for this template.

## HealthKit usage (basic per user's scope)

- **Steps:** primary success signal. A longevity user who walked 8000 steps today *trained today*, even without a formal session. The brain acknowledges this.
- **Sleep:** any night under 6 hours triggers a "lighter day today" automatic adjustment
- **Weight:** show trend on Becoming tab, but **do not emphasize**. Longevity users often shouldn't lose weight (sarcopenia risk in seniors). Weight matters less than function.
- **Heart rate / VO2max (out of scope):** would be ideal here but the user said "basic" — skip for v2

## Photo block

**Optional and off by default.** Longevity users don't want progress photos. They want quiet progress.

## Voice register tuning

Marcus base register, with the **largest deviation** of any template:

- Warmer than base. Longevity users have been condescended to by wellness culture; Marcus respects them but doesn't bark.
- Slower. Sentences are slightly longer (still short by general standards, but not staccato).
- Patient about progress. *"two weeks of holding this load. Right where you should be."* — never *"you should be progressing by now."*
- Honest about the goal: *"this isn't to look like a 25-year-old. it's to still be doing your own laundry at 85."*

Slip-day intervention for longevity:

> Missed Monday. Alright. Today: a 15-minute walk outside. That's the session. Wednesday we go back to the program.

Note: doesn't push for the missed lift. *Walking counts.* For other templates that would be too soft; for longevity it's the right call.

## What this template is NOT for

- Athletes (they need performance programming)
- People with active acute medical issues (immediate referral; not training)
- People who pick this because they're afraid of effort but are actually healthy — gentle redirect to Conditioning, which builds capacity rather than maintaining it

## Open questions for this template

- **Should we ask age explicitly?** Other templates don't. Longevity probably should — programming for a 55-year-old vs a 75-year-old differs. Age_band gets us there without exact age.
- **Should we partner with a clinical advisor?** Programming for older adults is sensitive. A medical advisor blessing the template's exercise selection would reduce liability and improve credibility. Not blocking v2 but worth pursuing.
- **What about post-rehab users?** Someone recovering from major surgery is technically not a longevity user but has overlapping needs. Probably a v2.1 separate template (`05b-rehab.md`); for now, longevity is the closest fit and the persona accepts injury notes carefully.
- **HRV / resting HR / readiness scoring.** Out of scope per user's "basic" HealthKit scope. Worth flagging as v2.1 because for this segment, readiness data is *more* useful than for athletes.
